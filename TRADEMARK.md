# Trademark policy

**"Ownpace"** is a trademark of **Archico B.V.** (Benelux trademark
application 1556706, filed 2026-08-30, registration pending; an EU
application follows). This policy exists so the one sentence that matters
is written down before anyone has to ask:

> **The code is free. The name is not.**

The Apache License 2.0 gives everyone the right to use, modify, distribute
and even sell this software — and its §6 expressly grants **no** trademark
rights. That split is deliberate. Ownpace moves people's mailboxes; the name
is how a user tells the service they trust from something that merely looks
like it.

## You do not need permission to

- **Run Ownpace**, self-hosted or modified, for yourself, your family, your
  company, or your clients — and say truthfully that you are running Ownpace.
- **Refer to the project by name**: articles, talks, courses, comparisons,
  package names in distributions that convey the software itself
  (`ownpace`, unmodified).
- Say your product or service is **"compatible with Ownpace"** or **"built
  on Ownpace"**, where that is true and does not read as endorsement.
- **Link to** ownpace.eu or this repository.

## Community and non-profit operators: a standing yes

One person running the appliance at home helps one household. A student
association, a library, a digital-rights group or a public-interest host
running it for their members helps hundreds — and that is the better
outcome, so the name should not stand in the way of it. This is therefore
a standing permission rather than a conversation.

**If you are a non-profit, an educational or public-sector body, or a
volunteer community**, you may run a hosted Ownpace instance for your
members and call it Ownpace, without asking us, as long as:

- **You say who runs it** — in the name and on the page: *"Ownpace,
  operated by «your organisation»"*, not "Ownpace" on its own.
- **You do not present it as ours**: not official, not endorsed, not
  certified, not the project's own service.
- **It is Ownpace you are running** — upstream or your own build of it —
  rather than a different product wearing the name.
- **It is not a commercial offering.** Recovering your costs, or asking
  members for a contribution, is fine; selling migrations at a margin is
  the section below, so talk to us first.
- **You link to the source** and keep the licence and notices intact.
- **You carry the support**: the people on your instance come to you, and
  we will hold neither their data nor their credentials.

No form, no fee, nothing to sign. Tell us if you like
(support@ownpace.eu) and we will gladly point at you.

**This is about the name only, and it takes nothing away from the code.**
Apache-2.0 lets anyone — including a company, including for profit — use,
modify, run and sell this software. Nothing on this page narrows that, and
nothing on this page is a condition of the licence. What is narrower is
the *name*, because the name is the only thing that tells a user whose
service is holding their mailbox.

The permission covers the name on your instance. It does not extend to
registering domains or company names built on it, app-store listings, or a
logo of your own resembling ours — those stay below. We can withdraw it
for a particular operator if a use turns out to confuse people about who
holds their data; we would talk to you first, because that confusion is
the only thing this policy exists to prevent.

## Please ask first (support@ownpace.eu) before you

- Name a **fork or derived product** "Ownpace" or something confusingly
  similar — ship your fork proudly, under your own name.
- Offer a **hosted or commercial service** under the name "Ownpace" or a
  confusingly similar name or domain — unless the standing permission
  above already covers you.
- Use the name or logo in a way that **implies endorsement, partnership, or
  official status** that does not exist.
- Register **domains, company names, social-media handles, or app-store
  listings** built on the name.

We expect to say yes to honest uses and no to confusing ones; the test is
always whether a user could mistake your thing for the service operated at
ownpace.eu.
