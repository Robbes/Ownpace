# A recommendation for the Alpha

Someone who already uses Ownpace has recommended you. You got a card or a link, with a code. With
that code you are let in to the Alpha at once.

## What a recommendation is {#what-a-recommendation-is}

Ownpace copies your mail, calendars, contacts and files to a European provider. You switch from
Google or Microsoft at your own pace. Until then Ownpace keeps the copy up to date.

Ownpace is still a trial. It is called the Alpha. Each person in it may recommend one person. They
tell us that they know you. That is enough: with the code you are let in at once.

You get an account of your own. The person who recommended you does not see what is in it. They
will see that the code was used.

## What it costs {#what-it-costs}

Ownpace charges nothing during the Alpha. Your new mailbox is at a European provider. You pay that
provider for it, not Ownpace.

After the Alpha there are paid packages and a free one. You pay nothing before you have accepted the
new conditions.

## Using your code {#using-your-code}

1. Open [the page for your code]([[REDEEM]]), and type the code from the card. Capitals and dashes
   do not matter.
2. Answer one question: did the person who gave you the card tell you about it themselves?
3. Fill in your email address. Then you are in.

Then you make your account with the sign-in service. It mails a code to your email address. With it
you confirm that the address is yours.

A code works once, until the date on the card. Does it not work? Then ask the person who gave you
the card for a new one.

Is your email address that of a Google account you want to move? Then say so on the page. We add
it to a list at Google by hand, and write to you when that is done. Until then Google does not allow
the connection.

## What we keep {#what-we-keep}

Of the recommendation we keep who made it, a fingerprint of the code (never the code itself), when
it was made and used, and which request it became. We never see your name or the message on the
card. What you fill in on the page stays with your account, and is erased with it. The
[privacy policy](./privacy.html) says more.
